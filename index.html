About the Lab

Who We Are

The C. elegans Research Group (Stress Biology Lab) at the Department of Genetics, ELTE, is dedicated to studying the molecular and cellular mechanisms that govern development, stress response, and aging using the model organism Caenorhabditis elegans. Our research integrates genetic, biochemical, and imaging techniques to explore fundamental questions in biology.

Led by Dr. János Barna, the group utilises advanced imaging, high-throughput genetics, and state-of-the-art genomic tools to answer questions related to stress response, aging, and neurodegenerative diseases. By understanding how cellular networks and signalling pathways govern lifespan and neuronal function, we aim to identify new therapeutic targets for age-related diseases.

Our research has broader implications for understanding human diseases such as Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, and cancer, where similar molecular mechanisms are often involved. Recent work demonstrates that HSF-1 — the master regulator of the heat shock response — also plays a critical role in activating the ER unfolded protein response, a finding conserved from C. elegans to human cells.

Research

Current Focus

Our primary research area is the biology of HSF-1 and its interactions with cellular stress response networks in C. elegans. Additional areas are under development — check back soon.

🧬
HSF-1 Biology & Stress Response Crosstalk

HSF-1 (Heat Shock Factor 1) is the master transcriptional regulator of the heat shock response — a universally conserved pathway that protects cells from proteotoxic damage. Traditionally, HSF-1 was viewed as responsible solely for upregulating cytoplasmic chaperones such as HSP-70 and HSP-90 to prevent protein misfolding.

Our lab has uncovered that HSF-1's roles extend well beyond this classical picture. We demonstrated that HSF-1 is required for the efficient activation of the endoplasmic reticulum unfolded protein response (UPRER) in C. elegans — linking the cytoplasmic heat shock response to the ER proteostasis machinery. We further showed that this connection is conserved in human cell lines, establishing HSF-1 as a key coordinator of integrated cellular stress responses across compartments.

A parallel discovery revealed a surprising hormetic phenomenon: inactivating HSF-1 paradoxically increases thermotolerance in young adult C. elegans by inducing compensatory activation of the UPRER and innate immunity pathways. This effect is strictly age-dependent — the same HSF-1 deficiency reduces thermotolerance in older worms — illuminating a dynamic interplay between stress response networks that declines with aging.

Ongoing work investigates the molecular mechanisms underlying HSF-1's regulation of UPRER, the roles of HSF-1 interactor proteins, and the broader network of HSF family member functions across development and aging.

Lab Techniques

Our research integrates classical C. elegans genetics with modern molecular biology and genomics approaches.

C. elegans Genetics & Maintenance

We maintain a broad collection of worm strains — loss-of-function mutants, RNAi knockdown lines, and transgenic animals. Age-synchronized populations allow developmental-stage–precise experiments.

Principal Investigator
JB
Dr. János Barna
Principal Investigator
Dr. János Barna is the Principal Investigator of the Stress Biology Lab at the Department of Genetics, Eötvös Loránd University, Budapest. He received his training in molecular biology and genetics, and has spent much of his career exploring how cells detect and respond to stress — with a particular focus on HSF-1, the master regulator of the heat shock response. Over the years, his work has revealed that HSF-1 plays a far broader role than previously appreciated, coordinating responses across the cytoplasm, the endoplasmic reticulum, and the immune system. Beyond the bench, Dr. Barna is deeply invested in mentoring the next generation of scientists and building a lab culture where curiosity and rigour go hand in hand. He is also affiliated with the HUN-REN–ELTE Genetics Research Group.
